My research in Information Science examines how information environments shape access, understanding, and decisions. I use computational and social-scientific methods to study local news, AI-mediated information, and public-interest questions in policy and health.

## Community Informatics Lab · George Mason University

*Undergraduate Research Assistant · December 2024–Present*
{: .role-date}

- Investigate **information inequality and news deserts**, examining local information environments, psychological distance, and civic information access through the collection and analysis of more than **40,000 news articles**.
- First author of [“Local news at a distance: Psychological distance and the formation of news deserts”](https://doi.org/10.47989/ir31iConf64264), published in *Information Research* for iConference 2026; co-author of two ASIS&T 2025 proceedings on local news coverage and spatial patterns.
- Conduct ongoing research on **AI-mediated local news**, examining how conversational interfaces may influence psychological distance, information access, and civic engagement.
- Contribute to **SAFETI**, a funded project conducted in collaboration with the Virginia Department of Labor and Industry, examining workplace safety, workers' compensation policies, and regulatory impacts with large-scale administrative data.

## Commonwealth Cyber Initiative · George Mason University

## Parkland Center for Clinical Innovation

*Data Science Intern · May–August 2026*
{: .role-date}

- Independently led an advanced research project, originally scoped for two interns, developing a **Behavioral Lifestyle Index** for Parkland Health.
- Worked with data covering approximately **500,000 patients and 9 million clinical encounters**.
- Developed a reproducible data-processing, feature-selection, and clustering methodology, narrowing more than 100 candidate variables to approximately 40 indicators with clinical input.
- Presented the research to clinical and data-science audiences, including a session with more than 200 attendees.

## Methods and tools

**Research methods:** Survey design and analysis; literature reviews and research synthesis; qualitative coding; policy evaluation; interdisciplinary study design.  
**Computational approaches:** NLP and LLM-assisted text analysis; geographic and social-network analysis; clustering; feature selection; predictive modeling; statistics; data visualization.  
**Programming and data:** Python (pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib), R, SQL, Spark, Java, C.  
**Research tools:** Qualtrics, Stata, Tableau, Git/GitHub, Jupyter, Linux/Unix.
